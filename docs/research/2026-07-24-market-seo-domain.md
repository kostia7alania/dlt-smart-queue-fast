# Market, SEO, and Domain Research

**Date:** 2026-07-24  
**Scope:** public, read-only Thai Department of Land Transport appointment
discovery  
**Evidence standard:** official documentation first; competitor statements are
treated as marketing claims; community posts are treated as qualitative signals,
not verified service facts.

## Executive Decision

Use **Thai Queue Scout** as the working public brand and
**`thaiqueuescout.com`** as the primary domain candidate.

Position it as:

> The independent way to find a workable Thai DLT appointment: compare offices,
> see recent availability, and finish booking on the official service.

The product should remain an availability and decision tool, not an unofficial
booking agent. Its durable advantages are:

- broad office discovery instead of one-office instructions;
- comparison, map, and historical evidence in one workflow;
- visible freshness and honest live/stored/error states;
- no account, identity document, or official-service credential collection;
- a clear hand-off to the official DLT booking flow;
- polite, bounded access to the upstream service.

Do not use `slot` in the brand or primary domain. Exact-brand searches produced
large amounts of Thai casino spam around `slot`, creating an avoidable trust,
brand-safety, and search-intent problem.

TDL Service publicly claims more than 4,000 expatriates helped and promotes an
end-to-end service. That indicates a willingness to pay for reduced uncertainty,
but it is not independent proof of market size or outcomes.

Schengen Alert is a particularly relevant operating analogue: it presents
itself as independent, alerts through WhatsApp, sells a time-bounded monitoring
period, makes users book on the official portal, and explicitly disclaims
government affiliation and the ability to bypass rules.

### User problem

The primary job is not “show me a calendar.” It is:

> Help me find the earliest realistic office/date combination without repeatedly
> checking a difficult official flow or paying for a full concierge service.

The strongest initial audiences are:

1. English-speaking residents converting or renewing a Thai driving licence.
2. Applicants in Bangkok who can travel to a nearby province for an earlier
   appointment.
3. Expat communities and local service providers answering repeated
   appointment questions.

Community discussions about long waits, using another province, and inconsistent
walk-in handling support the problem hypothesis, but those anecdotes conflict
and change quickly. Product copy must never turn them into universal policy.

## Category Conventions

Successful appointment-monitoring products commonly provide:

- a precise office, route, service, or date-range filter;
- a fast notification channel such as email, Telegram, WhatsApp, or push;
- freshness and coverage information;
- a direct link to complete the action on the official site;
- a limited monitoring period rather than an unclear permanent subscription;
- plain-language independence, no-guarantee, and refund terms;
- a useful free discovery layer before asking for payment.

For this product, the safe progression is:

1. Free read-only discovery and comparison.
2. Opt-in Telegram or email watchlists only after demand is measured.
3. A simple 30-day paid watch as the first monetization experiment.
4. No credential storage, captcha bypass, automatic booking, or artificial
   request acceleration.

## Positioning

### Category

Independent DLT appointment availability explorer.

### One-line value proposition

Find a practical Thai driving-licence appointment across offices before you
open the official booking service.

### Homepage message hierarchy

1. **Find an earlier Thai DLT appointment.**
2. Compare offices, map recent availability, and inspect slot history.
3. Unofficial and read-only. Availability can change; complete booking with DLT.
4. Primary action: **Check appointment availability**.
5. Secondary action: **Compare nearby offices**.

### Trust language

Always show:

- “Independent and not affiliated with Thailand's Department of Land
  Transport.”
- “Availability is informational and may change before you book.”
- “We do not book appointments or collect DLT account credentials.”
- data freshness and whether a result is live or stored.

Avoid:

- “official,” “guaranteed,” “reserved,” or “fast track”;
- claims that a particular office accepts every foreign applicant;
- fabricated review counts, urgency, or success rates.

## Search Architecture

Near-term indexable pages should be limited to content the product can keep
accurate:

```text
/
├── appointments
├── calendar
├── compare
├── map
├── guides
│   ├── dlt-smart-queue-for-foreigners
│   ├── convert-foreign-driving-license-thailand
│   └── renew-thai-driving-license
└── offices
    ├── bangkok
    ├── chiang-mai
    ├── pattaya
    └── phuket
```

The current interactive routes are useful but are not enough by themselves:
searchers also need a concise explanation, source date, caveats, and internal
links into a preselected live workflow. City pages should be introduced only
when they contain real office coverage and maintained local guidance.

Google's current guidance says that keywords in a domain have hardly any ranking
effect by themselves, meta keywords are unused, and keyword stuffing violates
spam policies. It also has an exact-match-domain system intended to prevent
excessive ranking credit. Domain choice should therefore optimize recognition,
trust, speech, and expansion rather than exact-query stuffing.

When the brand is final, use one consistent site name and `WebSite` JSON-LD on
the homepage. If Thai versions are introduced, use separate stable URLs,
reciprocal fully qualified `hreflang` links, and `x-default`.

## Domain Decision

### Criteria

- understandable when heard once;
- no government-affiliation implication;
- no gambling or adult-search ambiguity;
- broad enough for other Thai appointment systems later;
- short enough to type and use in a Telegram handle;
- `.com` preferred for international users, not for a supposed ranking bonus.

### Registry check

Verisign RDAP returned `404` (no current `.com` registry object) for every
candidate below on 2026-07-24. This is a point-in-time technical signal, not a
reservation or registrar guarantee.

| Candidate | Decision | Reason |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `thaiqueuescout.com` | **Primary** | Brandable, clear discovery metaphor, expandable, no DLT impersonation |
| `thaiqueuewatch.com` | Backup | Clear but sounds more like passive monitoring than active comparison |
| `thaidrivequeue.com` | Backup | Strong licence association but less expandable |
| `dltqueuescout.com` | Defensive only | Descriptive, but closer to an official-looking DLT identity |
| `dltqueuewatch.com` | Defensive only | Same affiliation risk and narrower category |
| `queuewatchthailand.com` | Reject | Understandable but long and awkward to say |

Before purchase:

1. Repeat RDAP and registrar checks.
2. Search Thai and English web, social, app-store, and trademark databases.
3. Confirm matching GitHub organization and social handles.
4. Buy the primary domain and one defensive redirect only if inexpensive.
5. Keep the legal/product disclaimer visible from launch.

## Measurement Plan

Create a dedicated property only after the production domain exists. Do not ship
the unrelated `Fiagdon` tag.

### Business funnel

```text
organic landing
  -> office or work option selected
  -> availability loaded
  -> compare/map/history used
  -> official booking link opened
  -> optional alert-interest signal
```

### Minimal events

| Event | Trigger | Useful parameters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `availability_search` | A user intentionally loads an office/work option | office ID, work type, result state, freshness |
| `office_compare` | A comparison completes | office count, result count, earliest-days bucket |
| `office_map_filter` | A meaningful map filter is applied | region/status, result count |
| `official_booking_click` | A user leaves for DLT | source page, office ID, work type |
| `alert_interest` | A user asks to be notified | channel preference, city/office count |

Never send names, document numbers, phone numbers, email addresses, full query
strings, or other personal data as analytics parameters. Use consent-aware,
first-party measurement appropriate to the launch jurisdictions. Search Console
query and landing-page data should become the main SEO feedback loop; GA should
answer what visitors do after landing.

### First decisions after real data arrives

- Double down on query/page pairs with impressions but weak click-through using
  clearer titles and snippets.
- Add content where real queries have product fit but no dedicated answer.
- Fix landing pages with clicks but low availability-search completion.
- Test alert demand before implementing paid monitoring.
- Do not infer demand from vanity page views alone.

## 90-Day Go-to-Market

### Before launch

- register the verified domain and set one canonical HTTPS host;
- deploy the static frontend and API using the repository runbook;
- add the domain to Search Console, submit `/sitemap.xml`, and inspect live URLs;
- create a dedicated analytics property and document consent behavior;
- add the independence/no-booking disclaimer and official hand-off;
- publish one excellent foreigner guide and one Bangkok comparison page.

### Days 1–30

- share the free tool in relevant expat communities without pretending to be a
  neutral community member;
- interview 10 users who recently tried to book;
- review Search Console queries weekly;
- measure official-booking clicks and alert-interest actions;
- correct guidance quickly when official behavior changes.

### Days 31–90

- publish the next two city pages only where the product has real coverage;
- build a no-payment alert-interest waitlist;
- validate channel preference and willingness to pay;
- if demand is real, test a bounded 30-day Telegram/email watch without
  credentials or auto-booking.
